# "The Emu War": DRAFT script, waiting for approval

**Idea:** in 1932 the Australian army sent soldiers with machine guns to stop emus eating farmers' wheat, and failed because the birds scattered and stayed out of range. The lesson: power doesn't help against a target that won't stand still.

**Length:** 177 words, about 53 s of speech, about 55 s with pauses. **Narrator:** Kokoro `am_liam` (version 5 voice) with the ta-ding ending.

**Title options:** "Australia Lost a War to Birds" / "The Soldiers Who Fought Emus... and Lost" / "The Great Emu War"

## Voice-over

Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight birds... and the birds won.

In 1932, a drought pushed about twenty thousand emus onto the wheat farms of Western Australia. Many of the farmers were veterans on poor land, and the birds were wrecking their wheat.

So the farmers asked for help, and the government sent the army.

But the emus did something nobody expected. They split into small groups, and ran.

Every time the soldiers got close, the birds scattered, and stayed just out of range.

So the army tried a truck. They mounted a machine gun on the back, and chased the birds. But a bouncing truck is a terrible place to aim.

You see, a machine gun works best on a target that stays in one place. Emus are fast, and they scatter.

The soldiers fired round after round, and the emus kept running.

So in the end, the army gave up and went home.

Power does not help when your target will not stand still.

The army had machine guns. The emus had legs.

## Rulebook check
- Hook first, no intro: a strange concrete image plus a payoff line ("and the birds won").
- "But" in shot 4, "Then" in shot 5, "So" in shots 6 and 9, "You see," as its own beat with a stop before and after (shots 7-8, at 64% of the runtime), a short closing line, no outro or call to action.
- No "?" or "!" in the narration. House style v2 applies (deep painted 3D scenes, a different camera angle in every shot, ta-ding ending).

## Left out on purpose (sources disagree or I could not verify)
- **Rounds fired and birds killed** (one account: 986 birds for about 9,860 rounds; others differ), and "wounded birds" claims.
- **The famous Meredith "tanks" quote** (no primary source found), any parliamentary debate, and any named ornithologist.
- **The exact withdrawal date.** One account has a withdrawal after about a week and a second round afterwards; the script only says the army gave up and went home.
- **Later bounties and later cull numbers.**

## Wording to watch
- The "painted ring" around the gun is an illustration of the guns' range, not a measured distance.
- The soldiers and the minister are shown as cartoon characters, not portraits of real people. Do not name Meredith or Pearce on screen.
- "Won" in the hook is tongue in cheek. The description should say the campaign was widely judged a failure.
