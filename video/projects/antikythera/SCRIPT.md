# "The Computer in the Shipwreck" (Antikythera mechanism): DRAFT script, waiting for approval

**Idea:** divers raised a corroded lump in 1901; inside was a 2,000-year-old geared machine that tracked the sun, moon, eclipses and the Olympic cycle. Nothing as complex is known for over a thousand years afterwards.

**Length:** 192 words, about 57 s of speech plus pauses (about 55 to 60 s). **Narrator:** Chatterbox B (as for the rat video), with the ta-ding ending.

**Title options:** "The 2,000-Year-Old Computer Found in a Shipwreck" / "Divers Found a Computer in a Shipwreck" / "The Machine That Shouldn't Exist"

## Hook options (I chose A)

- **A (chosen):** "Divers pulled a rusty lump off the sea floor... and inside it was a computer. Two thousand years old." Why: a concrete picture, a contradiction (rust vs computer), and a held-back number that lands as the shock.

- **B:** "In 1901, divers found a machine that should not have existed for another thousand years." More of a mystery, less visual.

- **C:** "Someone built a computer two thousand years ago, and then everyone forgot how." Stronger claim; I can't fully support the 'forgot how' part, so I'd avoid it.

## Voice-over

Divers pulled a rusty lump off the sea floor... and inside it was a computer. Two thousand years old.

In 1901, Greek sponge divers lifted treasure from an ancient shipwreck. Most of it was easy to understand. One piece looked like junk.

So it sat in a museum, and for a long time nobody knew what it was.

But when scientists finally looked inside with X-rays, they found gears.

More than thirty tiny bronze gears, all fitted together like the inside of a watch.

And that should have been impossible. Nothing this complicated was known for another thousand years.

You see, scans found hidden writing that explained how it worked. Turn the crank, and the gears moved pointers across the sky.

One dial showed the phases of the moon. Another warned of eclipses. A third counted the years between the Olympic games.

So around two thousand years ago, someone built a machine the size of a shoebox that could predict the sky.

Nobody knows who made it. Nothing like it appeared again for over a thousand years.

The ancient Greeks built a computer, and the sea kept it for two thousand years.

## Rulebook check
- First word at t=0, no intro. Hook is a strange concrete image plus a contradiction, with the shocking number ("two thousand years") held to the end of the hook.
- "But" in shot 4, "Then" in shot 5, the conflict ("impossible") in shot 6, and "You see," as its own beat with a stop before and after (shots 7 and 8), "So" in shot 10, a 13-word closing line, no outro or call to action.
- House style v2 applies: deep painted 3D scenes (underwater, a Greek harbour, a museum storeroom, a lab, a candle-lit workshop), different camera angle in every shot, soft shadows and the saved sound design.

## Left out on purpose
- **Exact construction date and the ship's exact sinking date.** Sources give 205 BC to 87 BC for the device and about 70 to 60 BC for the wreck. The script says "around two thousand years ago".
- **Exact gear count and the "all five planets" claim.** The back and front were only partly resolved, so the script says "more than thirty" and doesn't claim planets.
- **Statues in the cargo, the crank (it's lost), the museum "decades" claim, and any theory about who built it** (Archimedes, Hipparchus and others are only guesses).
- **"Everyone forgot how."** Not supportable.

## Wording to watch
- Say "hand-turned" and "a machine", not "the first computer" as a fact. Calling it an analogue computer is common, and the hook uses "computer" for impact. The description should say "often called the world's oldest analogue computer".
- The crank is not preserved. In the animation the scholar turns a crank as an illustration; the description should say that.
