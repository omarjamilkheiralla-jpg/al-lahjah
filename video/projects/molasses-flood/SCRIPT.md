# Style 1 short: "The Day Molasses Flooded Boston" (Great Molasses Flood, 15 Jan 1919)
Written and built in one go while the user was away (they said: pick a good true story and make it as stunning as possible).

Facts used (kept conservative): 15 January 1919, North End of Boston; steel tank about 50 ft tall, more than 2 million gallons of molasses; the tank leaked from the start and was painted brown instead of fixed (widely reported, e.g. Wikipedia/Puleo "Dark Tide"); safety checks such as a full water test were skipped; wave about 25 ft high at about 35 mph; 21 dead, about 150 injured; 119 plaintiffs sued US Industrial Alcohol, expert witnesses were used for the first time, and the case helped bring new rules for architects and engineers. Sources: Wikipedia, NPR (2019), Britannica, History.com, MIT Alumni "Solving the Great Molasses Flood mystery". The exact cause is still debated; the video does not claim one.

Voice-over: see script_lines.json (15 lines, "You see," beat on line 9, analogy = ignored dashboard warning light, last joke on the idiom "slow as molasses"). Tone: respectful about the 21 people, jokes only on the idiom and on the company's paint job.

Shots (no hard cuts, iris/dip/flash only): harbour hook, mild day street, huge tank, leaking seam + cup, painter, lunch + groan, burst, wave chase, wreckage, 21 lanterns, dashboard light, courtroom/engineer stamp, final sign + wave.
