# "How Chicago Lifted Itself" - full script (about 50 seconds)

**Format:** vertical 9:16, 174 words. The finished voice-over runs 52.3 s (about 3.3 words/s with pauses).
**Style rules used:** `video/STYLE_GUIDE.md` (hook formula "weird deliberate act", the 7-beat skeleton, "You see" pivot, hard stop on the payoff, no outro).
**Title options:** "Chicago Lifted Itself" / "The Hotel That Was Lifted With Guests Inside" / "Why Chicago Raised Its Streets"

## Voice-over (read as one piece)

A Chicago hotel was lifted six feet into the air with every guest still inside.

How is that even possible? Chicago sat barely above the lake, so its streets were mud and its sewers couldn't drain. After cholera hit in 1854, one engineer proposed raising the streets by up to fourteen feet.

But the buildings were already standing there, and nobody wanted to knock them down. Then a young builder named George Pullman said he could lift them with nothing but jackscrews.

The hotel's owner agreed on one condition: no guest could be disturbed.

You see, a jackscrew turns a tiny twist into a giant lift. So hundreds of men each took a screw.

Inch by tiny inch, they raised the hotel while guests kept eating and sleeping upstairs. So after the last turn, the hotel stood six feet higher, and not one guest had to leave.

Soon, whole blocks of shops were rising too, with the stores still open.

That's how Chicago lifted itself out of the mud, one tiny turn at a time.

## Left out on purpose (sources disagree or only one source)
- **The year of the Tremont House lift.** Sources give 1858, 1859, 1860 and 1861. Not stated in the script or on screen.
- **Exact numbers of men and jackscrews** (500 men / 5,000 jackscrews in one source, 600 / 6,000 in another). The script says "hundreds of men".
- **The exact height of the Lake Street block lift** (more than four feet in one source, six feet in another).
- **The cholera death toll**, a US senator staying at the hotel, and wagons sinking in the mud: no reliable source found.
- **Dialogue between Pullman and the owner is a dramatisation** based on the report that Pullman promised not to disturb guests.

## Wording to watch
- "Young builder": Pullman was about 30, fine, but do not call him an engineer.
- "Not one guest had to leave": sources say the hotel stayed occupied throughout.
- Say "about six feet", not an exact figure, if you add any on-screen text.

## Optional bonus line (not in the 50 s cut)
After the closing line you could add a fun fact in a longer cut: Pullman later put beds on trains and founded the Pullman Palace Car Company (confirmed on [George Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pullman)).
