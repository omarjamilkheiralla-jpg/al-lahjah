# Style 1 short (DRAFT, waiting for approval): "He Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."

Reference studied: "Why Cashiers Keep Winning The Lottery" (57.8 s, 720x1280, 30 fps, no hard cuts: the camera flows from one world into the next).
Length target: about 58 s, about 200 words. Voice: ElevenLabs (voice to be chosen when the key is in). Ending: ta-ding.

## Facts check (true story, Victor Lustig, Paris, 1925)
- 1925, con man Victor Lustig forged government papers and invited scrap metal dealers to a hotel (sources say six; the Hotel de Crillon is the usual name, we only say "a fancy hotel").
- He claimed the city could no longer afford to keep up the tower and would sell it for scrap. The Eiffel Tower really was built for the 1889 fair and meant to stand about twenty years.
- He picked André Poisson, a dealer who wanted to be somebody in Paris. Poisson paid (and also a bribe Lustig hinted at). Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police.
- Lustig came back later and tried it on a new group; this time one buyer went to the police (details differ by source, so we keep it short and do not name numbers).
- Sources: Smithsonian Magazine, Mental Floss, toureiffel.paris, Ripley's.

## Voice-over (about 200 words, numbers as words)
HOOK
In nineteen twenty five, a man sold the Eiffel Tower. Then he sold it again.

STORY
His name was Victor Lustig, and he was broke.
Everyone knew the tower was only built to stand for twenty years.
So Victor forged government papers, and invited six scrap metal dealers to a fancy hotel.
"Gentlemen, the city can't afford the tower. It will be sold for scrap. And this is a secret."
One dealer, André Poisson, wanted to be somebody in Paris. Victor saw it right away. He picked him.
Poisson paid for the whole tower, plus a little extra bribe to make sure he got it.
Victor took the cash and jumped on the first train out of the country.
Then he checked the newspapers. Nothing. Not one word.

YOU SEE
You see, Poisson never told the police. He was too embarrassed.
Think of the last time you got scammed online. Did you tell everyone? Or did you delete the email and never speak of it again?
That silence is how scams survive.

PAYOFF
A few months later, Victor came back to Paris, and sold the tower again, to a brand new group of dealers.
This time, one of them went straight to the police. Victor ran.
The Eiffel Tower, for the record, was never told it had been sold twice.

## Shot list (our painted characters, deep painted 3D Paris, richer textures and lighting, smooth camera)
Rule from the reference: NO hard cuts. The camera keeps moving and flows into the next place (push through an object, pull back from a close-up, fall into a top-down view, circular iris), word-by-word captions, rack focus (blurred background), a different camera angle every shot, warm lamp light with deep shadows.
1. Hook line. Frame 0: low camera sliding along a Paris street at dusk, the tower huge behind, a small man in a long coat looks up at it and tips his hat. Slow push up the tower. Caption word by word.
2. "Then he sold it again": camera rises over the top of the tower and flips to a second tiny tower on a "SOLD" sign (visual joke), then whips down into the next shot.
3. Victor in a tiny attic room, empty pockets turned out, a bare lamp swinging, bread crust on a plate. Camera orbits him from above, then drops to his face (rack focus).
4. Street view of the tower with a worn "20 YEARS" plaque; pigeons; a worker painting it. Slow dolly across.
5. Top-down desk shot: Victor stamps fake papers, six envelopes with six names slide in. Camera tilts up to his grin.
6. Grand hotel salon, six scrap dealers in caps and aprons around a table, camera low and sweeping through their legs and chairs. Victor stands, lamp behind him.
7. Close-up of Victor whispering "this is a secret" (finger on lips). Camera pushes in to an extreme close-up of his eye, then pulls out through the eye into the next shot (dealers leaning in).
8. Poisson's face: eager, shiny, a hat that is too big. Camera circles him slowly while the others fade to blur.
9. Hands: a thick roll of cash and a second smaller roll slide across the table. Top-down, then camera follows the cash into a suitcase.
10. Train platform: steam, Victor leaps onto a moving train with the suitcase. Side tracking shot along the train.
11. Newspaper stand, camera slides along rows of papers, headlines about weather and football. "Nothing."
12. YOU SEE beat: Poisson alone in a dark room, head in hands, tower silhouette in the window. Slow push-in, no sound effect on "You see,".
13. Modern vignette: a person at a laptop deleting an email, camera crane up and out of the room into the night sky, then back down to Paris.
14. Victor returns: same station, new dealers, same speech. Camera whips through a blurred crowd.
15. A dealer runs into a police station; police whistles; Victor sprints over rooftops in a comic long shot, camera swings above.
16. Last joke: huge tower at night with two "SOLD" signs hanging from it, camera pulls far back; the tower (with tiny eyes made from two lit windows) looks confused. ta-ding, hold 1.5 s, hard stop.
