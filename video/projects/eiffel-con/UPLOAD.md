## Description
In 1925, a broke con man named Victor Lustig sold the Eiffel Tower. Then, a few months later, he sold it again.

This is the true story of how he did it: forged government papers, six scrap metal dealers, one very embarrassed buyer, and a trick that still works on people today. Why do scams survive? Because the people who get fooled are too embarrassed to tell anyone.

Think of the last time you got scammed online. Did you tell everyone, or did you delete the email and never speak of it again?

The Eiffel Tower, for the record, was never told it had been sold twice.

Story sources: Smithsonian Magazine, Mental Floss, the official Eiffel Tower website (toureiffel.paris), Ripley's. Accounts differ on small details such as the hotel and exact numbers, so this video keeps to what they agree on. Animated for entertainment.
