# "She Fell Two Miles Out of the Sky" (true story: Juliane Koepcke, LANSA Flight 508, 1971)

## Hook
On Christmas Eve, nineteen seventy-one, a seventeen-year-old girl fell two miles out of the sky, still buckled into her seat.
She landed in the middle of the Amazon rainforest. Alone.

## The flight
That morning, Juliane and her mom got on a plane in Lima, Peru, to fly over the jungle and spend Christmas with her dad.
Half an hour in, they flew straight into a storm.
Lightning hit the wing, and the plane broke apart in the air.
The last thing she heard was her mom saying, "That's the end. It's all over."

## Day one
Juliane wakes up on the jungle floor, still strapped to her row of seats.
Her collarbone is broken, one eye is swollen shut, and she has lost her glasses and one shoe.
Her mom is gone. All she can find is a small bag of candy.

## Day two
But Juliane knows something most people don't.
Her parents were scientists who studied this exact jungle, and her dad taught her one rule.
"If you're ever lost, find water. Small streams lead to bigger rivers, and rivers lead to people."
She finds a tiny stream, and she starts walking.

## Day five
She wades down the river, poking the water with a stick to scare away stingrays.
Rescue planes fly right over her head. They can't see her through the trees.
The candy runs out.

## Day nine
A cut on her arm is now full of maggots, and she can barely stand.
Then, on the riverbank, she sees a boat, and a path, and a little wooden hut.
Inside, she finds a can of gasoline, and she pours it on her arm.
Thirty maggots crawl out. She counts them.

## The turn
You see, the safest thing she could have done looked like staying at the crash.
But under trees that thick, nobody would ever have found her.
One sentence from her dad got her moving.

## Day ten
The next day, three loggers walk into the hut and find a girl covered in mud and cuts.
She explains, very politely, that she fell out of a plane.
They take her down the river, and she is flown to her dad.

## Ending
Juliane was the only survivor out of ninety-two people on board.
Today, she is a biologist, and she runs her parents' research station.
In the same jungle.
