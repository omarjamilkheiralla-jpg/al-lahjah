# What if humans could not lie? (6-minute day-by-day what-if)

Status: SCRIPT FOR APPROVAL (nothing built yet).

Words: 910 (about 5.4 min with pauses)

## Voice-over

### HOOK

Tomorrow morning, every human on Earth wakes up unable to lie.

Not one lie. Not even a small one.

And by the end of the week, the world will look nothing like it did.

### DAY ONE

Day one. Your neighbor asks, how are you? You try to say, fine. But you are not fine. And the word will not come out. So you say, I am exhausted, and I am scared about money. Your neighbor goes pale. Because they just said the same thing.

Studies suggest the average person tells about one or two lies a day. Most are small and polite. Now they are gone.

A coworker shows you a new haircut. You say, it looks bad. You can not take it back. Across the world, politeness breaks down.

Phone calls go badly too. Sorry, I can not come, I am busy, becomes, I just do not want to. Invitations stop. Group chats go silent.

### DAY TWO

Day two. Work becomes a minefield. In every job interview, someone asks, what is your biggest weakness? And you have to give the real answer.

Salespeople panic first. Is this the best price? No. Will it break? Probably. Advertisers go quiet, because a slogan like, the best coffee in the world, can only be said by someone who truly believes it.

Bosses find out what their workers really think of them. Some offices are empty by lunch.

In hospitals, doctors can no longer say, it will be fine, unless they believe it. Patients learn the real odds. Some are grateful. Some are crushed.

### DAY THREE

Day three. The courts. No witness can lie under oath. No suspect can say, I did not do it.

So they learn the oldest trick there is. Say nothing. Lawyers tell their clients to stay silent. Police learn that a quiet room tells them almost nothing.

Meanwhile, the people who were lying to protect a secret are suddenly terrified of every question.

Scammers, who live on lies, lose their best tool overnight. The phone calls stop. The fake offers stop. For one strange day, the world feels safer.

### DAY FOUR

Day four. Love. Do you still love me? Was it true, what you said on our wedding day? Couples who were pretending are suddenly honest. Some find they are happier than they have ever been. Others split up on the spot.

Friends ask each other the questions they were always afraid of. And get answers.

A grandmother can not say she loves the gift she hates. A child can not say sorry, if they are not sorry. Families discover how much of their peace was built on small lies.

### DAY FIVE

Day five. Politics. A reporter asks, will you raise taxes? The candidate can not say no if the answer is yes. So the speeches stop. Leaders answer, I do not know, or they answer nothing at all.

Online, influencers can not say, I use this every day, unless it is true. People who share what they really think discover how divided they are.

Countries can not bluff. Negotiators who need to hide their offer have to stay silent. The old game of diplomacy, built on what you hide and what you show, breaks down.

But this silence is a trap.

### DAY SIX

Day six. Games and surprises are gone. A poker table is just people sitting quietly. A surprise party is impossible. If a friend asks, are you planning something? You have to say yes.

And then, something strange happens. People are afraid of each other. Not because anyone is lying. Because everyone is telling the truth.

A child asks, is Santa real? A parent has to answer. And across the world, thousands of kids get the news early.

### DAY SEVEN

Day seven. The world has found a way through.

You see, not lying is not the same as telling everything. You can refuse to answer. You can stay silent. You can be wrong, honestly. And you can still mislead, using words that are completely true.

People learn to ask better questions. They learn to say less. They learn to say true things kindly. Trust rises. But so does a new kind of loneliness. Because the people who speak the most are the people who are hurt the most.

### ONE YEAR LATER

One year later, a new rule of manners has appeared. When a question is too sharp, people answer, I would rather not say. It becomes the most common sentence on Earth.

Honesty did not make everyone brave. It made everyone careful.

### THE TWIST

Here is what nobody expects. Many of the things we love are built on lies. A magic trick. A bluff. A surprise party. A kind word to a friend who is hurting, even when we are not sure it is true.

Some argue stories would survive, because everyone knows a story is made up. But the small lies that smooth our days, the ones we tell a dozen times without thinking, would be gone. And we would have to replace them with something harder. The skill of telling the truth, and being kind at the same time.

### THE END

So would a world without lies be better? Maybe. Honesty can heal. It can also cut.

The truth is, we do not want to know everything about each other. We want to choose what we say, and when.

A world without lies would not be a world without secrets. It would be a world of silence.

The lie was never the real problem. The real problem is what we do with the truth.

## Fact notes
- Lying studies: diary studies found about one lie a day for community adults and about two for students; a later national survey found an average of 1.65 a day, with most lies told by a small group of frequent liars. The script says "about one or two lies a day, mostly small and polite".
- Everything else is a clearly framed hypothetical ("imagine every human could not lie"), not a claim about the real world. No made-up statistics.
- Wording to watch: the rule I use is "can not lie, but can stay silent, be honestly wrong, or mislead with true words". That keeps the story logical.
