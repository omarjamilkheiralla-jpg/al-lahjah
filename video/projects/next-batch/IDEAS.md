# Next batch: 7 story ideas (history, theory, facts). Not scripted yet; waiting for approval.

| # | Story | Type | Hook (first line) | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The planes with no bullet holes (Abraham Wald, WWII) | Theory + history | "The army wanted to armor the planes where the bullet holes were. A mathematician said that was exactly wrong." | Survivorship bias: the missing planes are the ones that were hit in the weak spots. |
| 2 | The dancing plague of 1518 (Strasbourg) | History mystery | "In 1518, a woman started dancing in a street, and she could not stop." | Dozens to hundreds joined; cause still debated (mass psychogenic illness is the leading theory). |
| 3 | The corpse that fooled an army (Operation Mincemeat, 1943) | History | "Britain gave a dead man a fake life, and the enemy believed every word." | A body with planted papers washed up in Spain and pushed the enemy to guard the wrong coast. |
| 4 | The road that made traffic worse (Braess's paradox) | Theory | "Adding a road can make everyone's trip longer." | Everyone choosing their own fastest route can leave the whole network slower. |
| 5 | The war against the emus (Australia, 1932) | History | "Soldiers with machine guns went to war against birds. The birds won." | The emus scattered and the army pulled out; farmers later used rifles and bounties. |
| 6 | The tiny hole in every airplane window | Fact | "Look at your airplane window. That hole is the only thing keeping it safe." | A bleed hole in the middle pane makes the outer pane carry the cabin pressure, and reduces fogging. |
| 7 | The computer from a 2,000-year-old shipwreck (Antikythera mechanism) | History | "Divers pulled up a lump of corroded bronze. Inside was a computer." | Gears that tracked the sun, moon, eclipses and the Olympic cycle. |

## Sources and caveats (what the script may and may not say)
1. **Wald.** Core is documented: he worked in the Statistical Research Group at Columbia and pointed out that only returning planes could be studied. [We Are The Mighty](https://www.wearethemighty.com/history/abraham-wald-survivor-bias-ww2), [AMS Feature Column](https://www.ams.org/samplings/feature-column/fc-2016-06). **Caveat:** the popular version ("armor where there are no holes") is partly legend. Say "the story goes", and stick to the reasoning, not specific numbers or planes.
2. **Dancing plague.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_plague_of_1518), [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/dancing-plague-medieval-strasbourg/). **Caveats:** the first dancer's name (Frau Troffea) is uncertain; the number of dancers (50 to 400) and any death toll are disputed. Say "dozens, maybe hundreds" and present causes as theories (ergot is doubted).
3. **Mincemeat.** [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat), [History Extra](https://history.co.uk/article/operation-mincemeat). Safe facts: planted papers on a body, recovered at Huelva on 30 April 1943, meant to point away from Sicily. **Caveats:** the man's identity and cause of death are disputed; the exact effect is debated. Don't name or describe him in detail.
4. **Braess.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braess%27s_paradox). **Caveats:** publication year is 1968 or 1969 depending on source; the Stuttgart and Seoul examples are widely repeated but thinly documented. Teach the logic as the story, and call examples "reported".
5. **Emu War.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu_War), [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/the-great-emu-war/). **Caveats:** rounds fired and birds killed vary by source; avoid exact numbers. Safe: Nov-Dec 1932, a few soldiers with Lewis guns, about 20,000 emus, the army withdrew.
6. **Window hole.** [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/why-is-there-a-hole-in-airplane-windows-1703660371). Safe: the hole equalises pressure so the outer pane carries the load; secondary role against fogging.
7. **Antikythera.** [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/1334586), [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/calculators/1/42). Safe: raised in 1901, at least 30 surviving gears, tracks eclipse cycles and the four-year games cycle. **Caveats:** dating varies (about 1st century BC); avoid a precise date and avoid the unreliable "suppressed knowledge" claims.
