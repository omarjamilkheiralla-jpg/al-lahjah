# "What if every house in town got the same mystery letter?"

## Hook
What if every house in one small town got the same letter on the same morning?
No stamp. No name on the envelope. Just one line inside.
"Someone in this town is not who they say they are."
Three thousand people read it before breakfast.

## Monday
Monday morning, the whole town stands on the sidewalk, holding the same piece of paper.
Nobody says a word.
Then, very slowly, everybody turns and looks at their neighbor.
Mister Henderson waves. Too friendly. Suspicious.

## Tuesday
On Tuesday, people start checking.
The barber gets asked for his birth certificate in the middle of a haircut.
Somebody pulls the mayor's mustache. It comes right off.
He says it is for a play. There is no play.

## Wednesday
On Wednesday, the town hall opens a desk with a sign that says "Who Are You Really?"
The line goes around the block twice.
The French baker admits his real name is Gary, and he is from Ohio.
His croissants come out of a frozen bag. The crowd gasps.
A sweet old grandma confesses she has been cheating at bingo for forty years.

## Thursday
On Thursday, the hardware store sells out of cameras by lunch.
Every porch gets one. Every window gets two.
All the cameras point at the neighbors, and the neighbors' cameras point right back.
Missus Patel watches eleven screens at once and forgets to eat dinner.

## Friday
But on Friday, someone finds a real clue.
A giant muddy footprint outside the post office, size fourteen.
The whole town lines up to compare shoes.
It belongs to the police chief. He says he was mailing a birthday card to his mom.
Nobody believes him.
That night, the neighborhood watch starts watching the neighborhood watch.

## Saturday
On Saturday, the town holds a vote to find the stranger.
One vote per house. Three thousand votes.
Three thousand different names.
Every single person voted for the house next door.

## The turn
You see, the letter never said who it was.
So everybody went looking, and everybody found something.
Psychologists call it confirmation bias: once you suspect something, you start seeing proof everywhere.

## Sunday
On Sunday morning, the mailman finally talks.
The letter was never a warning.
It was an ad for the new murder mystery dinner at the diner.
The second page, the one with the price, got stuck in the printer.
Tickets are twenty dollars.
They sell out in four minutes.
And Gary is doing the dessert. From a bag.
